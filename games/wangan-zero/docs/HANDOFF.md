## What Wangan Zero is
1. A **cockpit-first, night bay-expressway racing game** — the fictional Kurohama Bay at 02:13.
2. Genre inspiration: wangan / Shutokō night-running culture (Wangan Midnight / Tokyo Xtreme Racer lineage). Japanese subtitle on the title is **湾岸ゼロ**.
3. Current build is an **open-cruise speed/feel prototype**: Reimei XR as the player car, one looping straight-line physical route, clutch-gated sequential shifting, procedural Canvas-2D visuals and Web-Audio sound, plus visual-only shallow road curves, light civilian traffic, five persistent ambient rivals, timed/signaled lane changes, collisions, drafting, a first rear-view mirror, and an in-console loop GPS. No mission objective, duel prompt, economy, or saves yet.
4. Everything is **original/fictional** by design (cars, badges, place names, music). Keep it that way; do not add real marques or copy real compositions.

## Current mechanics snapshot for next session
Use this section as the quick behavioral truth before changing code:

1. **Player drive model:** `drivingModel.ts` owns speed, gears, RPM, clutch pressure/free-rev behavior, route distance, draft power input, and reusable `DrivePerformanceProfile` tuning. Reimei naturally settles near `321 km/h`; boost can reach but never exceed the `348 km/h` hard limit. The route loops at `7200 m`.
1a. **Cosmetic burnout:** `burnoutModel.ts` observes clutch/RPM/gear/speed without feeding the drive model. It arms only below `40 km/h`, in first, with clutch held and RPM at least `5,000`; release starts a `1.5 s` smoke/squeal effect. `burnoutVisual.ts` is a reusable rear-wheel-coordinate painter used by the mirror and ready for a future remote-car render. No multiplayer transport/runtime exists yet.
2. **Traffic count is intentionally tiny:** exactly one sedan and one container truck. Do not increase density unless the design explicitly asks for it.
3. **Traffic speeds:** sedans cruise at `75.6–100.8 km/h` and hard-cap at `180`; trucks cruise at `58.8–78.4 km/h` and hard-cap at `120`. Donated speed bleeds back toward cruise over the following seconds. Spawn lanes are weighted by vehicle kind: trucks mostly left, sedans mostly center.
4. **Rivals:** exactly one of each of five ambient rival identities exists. Session creation randomizes their loop positions, lanes, and definition-bounded cruise speeds. They move continuously off-screen, never recycle around the player, activate once visible, and range from white Shirokage's `221 km/h` normal cap through Kagero VX's `321 km/h`; slipstream headroom is mechanically capped at `+18 km/h`.
5. **Rival lane AI:** the rival scans all three lanes. If a safe escape lane exists, it starts a one-lane signal toward that route and keeps free-running speed while signaling. It brakes only when no viable lane move is available and it is boxed in.
6. **Lane changes:** player, sedan traffic, and rivals use `LaneChangeIntent`. Blinkers pulse at `0.0 s` and `0.5 s`; collision/draft committed lane changes at `1.0 s`. Visual `laneFraction` can move before the committed lane changes.
7. **Blinker visuals:** player blinkers are cockpit/dashboard triangles. Traffic/rival blinkers are mostly opaque amber horizontal triangles anchored near the lamp area and pointing toward the intended lane, rendered in windshield and rear-view mirror.
8. **Drafting:** player, traffic, and rivals use the same `draftModel.ts`. Same-lane trailing vehicles draft the nearest vehicle ahead. A useful light wake begins just inside `400 m`, crosses half distance strength around `105 m`, reaches maximum at `1 m`, and requires the front vehicle to be at least `60 km/h`.
9. **Draft visuals:** active player draft shows a compact right-side `DRAFT +N%` chip; the old road-space brackets and gap/risk copy remain intentionally removed.
10. **Collisions:** collision is lane-discrete and longitudinal. Every player, rival, sedan, and truck uses the same mass-weighted closing-speed exchange, hard-speed limits, and no-pass-through separation. Soft player/rival nudges feed draft trains; civilians recover toward cruise. Lateral side-swipe collision during the lane-change window is not implemented yet.
11. **Rendering:** road curves are visual only. Do not feed route curve into physics, traffic, rival AI, collision, or draft logic.
12. **No duel system yet:** there is no rival challenge prompt, duel alignment, duel score/health bar, rewards, economy, garage, save state, or multiplayer runtime.

## Architecture / key files
All paths are under `games/wangan-zero/`.

1. `src/main.ts` — app bootstrap: creates canvas + `Renderer2D` + `InputManager`, wires `BootScene` ↔ `GameScene`, runs the engine `FixedLoop`. Also attaches `TouchDriveControls`.
2. `src/context.ts` — `AppServices` (`renderer`, `input`) passed to scenes.
3. `src/types.ts` — `GAME_MANIFEST` (id/name/size/fps), `GAME_TITLE_JP` (`湾岸ゼロ`), `GAME_TAGLINE`.
4. `src/drivingModel.ts` — **pure, testable** physics + looping route model: gear/speed/rpm bands, `driveAccelerationKphPerSecond`, `stepDriveModel`, `ROUTE_SECTORS`, `getRouteSector`, `clamp`, constants (`MAX_GEAR`, `ROUTE_LENGTH_METERS`, …). No rendering here. `DriveState.finished` is retained for compatibility but remains `false`; the route loops instead of ending.
5. `src/drivingModel.test.ts` — Vitest for the model. **The sector-selection test asserts sector ids by distance — update it if you change `ROUTE_SECTORS`.**
5a. `src/burnoutModel.ts`, `src/burnoutModel.test.ts` — pure presentation trigger/timer and boundary tests. It deliberately exposes no force, grip, speed, or acceleration output. `src/burnoutVisual.ts` draws the two-wheel smoke sprite at caller-supplied screen coordinates.
6. `src/trafficModel.ts`, `src/trafficModel.test.ts` — pure relative-motion traffic state, sedan/truck kind, slower per-kind speed bands, weighted spawn lanes, delayed sedan lane-change/follow AI, recycling, visibility, and tested player-relative `0°`/`4°` sprite selection. `trafficYawFrame` retains the old disabled sedan far/close swap.
6b. `src/rivalModel.ts`, `src/rivalModel.test.ts` — pure ambient rival state/AI: five definition-owned speed/acceleration/aggression/braking personalities, randomized persistent closed-loop positions, high-speed reserve, peer-aware three-lane choice, boxed-in braking/following, and shared draft acceleration.
6e. `src/routeMap.ts`, `src/routeMap.test.ts` — pure normalized closed-loop route geometry plus sector/progress interpolation for the cockpit GPS; future rival and remote-player markers should reuse `routeMapPositionAt`.

## Invariants — do not break these
1. **Road perspective (`GameScene.projectRoad`).** Width MUST share the vertical's curve, while visual-only route shape may offset projected center only:
   - `screenDepth = depth ** ROAD_PERSPECTIVE_EXP`
   - `y = HORIZON_Y + screenDepth * (ROAD_BOTTOM_Y - HORIZON_Y)`
   - `centerX = screenCenter + visualCurveOffset`
   - `halfWidth = (ROAD_FAR_HALF_WIDTH + screenDepth * (ROAD_NEAR_HALF_WIDTH - ROAD_FAR_HALF_WIDTH)) * speedFov`
   Using a different exponent for width than for `y` reintroduces the old bowed perspective and detaches the kerb stripes. `ROAD_FAR_HALF_WIDTH` is the apex width; lower it and the road pinches to a needle again. Everything on the road (lane markers, kerb stripes, roadside lamps, guardrail, and forward traffic) reads from `projectRoad`, so this one function controls both perspective and the visual-only curve.
2. **Tunnel art never occupies asphalt or vehicle pixels.** The base enclosure draws in `renderBackdrop` before `renderRoad`. `renderTunnelForeground` is the deliberate exception to the old all-behind-road order: it redraws only opaque side-wall pixels through `traceTunnelWallPath`, outside the projected asphalt, after roadside geometry and before traffic. Keep vehicles after that pass, keep the clip outside `halfWidth + 2`, and never add a full-screen tunnel/gantry overlay.
3. **Engine boundary rules** (repo-wide): games import only from `@playloom/*` packages; never `games/* → games/*`; never `packages/* → games/*`. Keep lore/HUD/balance/theme inside this folder.
4. **Web-Audio needs a user gesture.** Runtime audio classes build their graphs on `unlock()`, fired from the first keydown/pointerdown. `M` / touch AUDIO must continue applying one shared mode to engine/SFX and `NightVelocityMusic`; don't try to start audible playback before interaction.
5. **Traffic/rival lanes and the player.** `TrafficLane` is `left | center | right`, mapped by `laneRoadFraction` to road fractions `-2/3`, `0`, `+2/3`. The **player occupies its committed current lane (`LANE_ORDER[playerLaneIndex]`) at `relativeMeters` 0** — the reference frame collisions assume; pass that lane to `resolveRoadVehicleCollisions` (or the traffic-only wrapper). A pending `LaneChangeIntent` can visually move the car/camera before the committed lane changes; collision and draft still use the committed lane until the intent completes. Traffic and rivals may use any of the three lanes. `trafficSpriteView` is the forward windshield sprite-direction source of truth for every non-player road vehicle: same lane → rear `00°`; player right of vehicle → regular rear `04°`; player left of vehicle → mirrored rear `04°`. `rearViewTrafficSpriteView` is the mirror source of truth: same lane → front `00°`; vehicle right of player → regular front `04°`; vehicle left of player → mirrored front `04°`. Curves are visual only; do not put them into `trafficModel`, `rivalModel`, or `collision.ts`. Keep forward road vehicles behind the cockpit and sort them far-to-near.
6. **Centered camera / lane offset.** The cockpit is fixed at screen center; lateral movement is faked by sliding the world with `laneShift(halfWidth)` added to **every** road/traffic/rival screen x. If you add a new on-road element, feed its x through `laneShift` too or it will not pan with the lane change. `laneShift` must scale with the road half-width at that depth to stay perspective-correct.
7. **Open route loop.** `stepDriveModel` wraps `distanceMeters` with modulo `ROUTE_LENGTH_METERS` and never marks a run complete. Do not reintroduce a mission finish overlay unless the design explicitly changes back from open cruising.

## The sector / backdrop system (Task most likely to be extended next)
- Route sectors are authored in `drivingModel.ts` → `ROUTE_SECTORS` (id, label, startMeters, accent). Current ids: `downtown`, `bridge`, `harbor`, `mountains`, `tunnel`, `skyline`. The `RouteSector["id"]` union type is the source of truth; every id must have an entry in `SECTOR_THEMES`, `ROAD_VISUAL_PROFILES`, and `ROUTE_MAP_SECTOR_PATHS`.

- `GameScene.ROAD_VISUAL_PROFILES` maps each sector id → visual-only shallow-curve tuning. Current feel: downtown flat, bridge gentle sweep, harbor level waterfront, mountains long shallow bend, tunnel nearly straight, skyline level return. `roadVisualProfileAt()` blends profiles around sector boundaries and over the final `LOOP_MORPH_DISTANCE` so the route wraps smoothly back into downtown.
- Road curve wave phase is based on continuous full-route progress (`routeProgressAt(worldDistance)`), not per-sector progress. This avoids visible road kinks when the upcoming `2400 m`, `3600 m`, and `4800 m` sector boundaries enter the `ROAD_DRAW_DISTANCE` look-ahead window.

## Feel / tuning knobs (all in `drivingModel.ts`)

- **Road-line speed drama:** `ROAD_LINE_SCROLL_MULTIPLIER` in `GameScene.ts` is `1.35`. `roadLineDistanceMeters()` applies it only to road seams, lane dashes, and edge stripes, leaving physics, traffic, and general parallax unchanged.
- **Route loop length:** `ROUTE_LENGTH_METERS` and `ROUTE_SECTORS` in `drivingModel.ts`. Keep the route open-ended by wrapping distance instead of setting `finished`.
- **Visual-only road shape:** `ROAD_VISUAL_PROFILES`, `ROAD_SEGMENT_LENGTH`, `HORIZON_Y`, `ROAD_FAR_HALF_WIDTH`, and `ROAD_NEAR_HALF_WIDTH` in `GameScene.ts`. These affect drawing only; physics remains a straight three-lane reference frame.
- **Per-car pull / gear spread:** `REIMEI_XR_PERFORMANCE.gearTorqueMultipliers` and `.gearSpeedLimitsKph` (index = gear, `MAX_GEAR = 6`). Future cars should define another `DrivePerformanceProfile` rather than add speed-specific branches.
- **Top speed:** `REIMEI_XR_PERFORMANCE.naturalTopSpeedKph` (`321`) sets the aerodynamic reference; `.hardSpeedLimitKph` (`348`) is the absolute boost cap. Base power and aerodynamic drag are balanced so ordinary full throttle settles around 321.
- **Rev dependence / low-end:** `enginePowerFactor` — the constant term is the off-idle floor (raise = low revs pull more), the exponent shapes how sharply power climbs with revs. Constrained by the `enginePowerFactor(...)` monotonicity test and the sequential-vs-5th test.
- **Base power / drag / rev limits:** performance profile fields own base power/gearing/aero; `driveAccelerationKphPerSecond` owns shared rolling drag, braking, over-rev braking, and `REV_LIMITER_RPM` behavior.
- **Drafting:** `src/draftModel.ts` owns `DRAFT_MIN_DISTANCE_METERS` (`1`), `DRAFT_MAX_DISTANCE_METERS` (`400`), `DRAFT_HALF_EFFECT_DISTANCE_METERS` (`105`), `DRAFT_MIN_FRONT_SPEED_KPH` (`60`), and `DRAFT_MAX_POWER_MULTIPLIER` (`1.75`).
